Single continuous cinematic shot, no cuts. The camera plunges from above a colossal arctic fortress city carved into a glacier — towers of black ice and frozen stone stacked impossibly high, aqueducts locked mid-flow. It dives toward a faint streak of light screaming along a narrow frozen aqueduct-road hugging the outer wall. Lock-on: a bone-white jet sled, low to the ground, rear thrusters burning electric blue, hull vibrating against the ice surface. The camera slingshots ahead, whips back, drops tight to the rear — ice shards snapping upward, thruster heat warping the frozen air, warning beacons flashing red along the wall. A frozen bridge cracks and collapses mid-crossing; the sled snaps left onto a maintenance ledge, grinding sparks off the wall. The camera threads through the same gap a half-second behind. Blizzard whiteout swallows everything for one beat — then the sled punches through, weaving between hanging ice-chain barriers and frozen archways with inches to spare. One final bend, then sudden release — the camera blasts outward into a massive reveal: the fortress city opens onto a boundless glacial valley, ice cliffs cascading into frozen waterfalls, pale arctic light breaking through storm clouds into cold gold, mist rising off the ice in slow silence.
