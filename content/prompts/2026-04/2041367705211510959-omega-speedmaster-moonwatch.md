OMEGA SPEEDMASTER MOONWATCH
"BORN QUALIFIED"

65mm IMAX. Ultra-wide anamorphic Panavision. Film grain building progressively — starts clean, ends maximum. Key light: cold white — mission control fluorescent. Secondary: warm capsule interior amber. Flares: from the hesalite crystal and the tachymeter bezel edge — unique oval anamorphic signature. Background: Omega factory → Mission Control Houston → lunar surface at reveal.
00:00–00:02 · THE CALIBRE 321
Inside the movement at microscopic scale. The column wheel of the chronograph mechanism — the most complex component — assembles first. Each column rising from the plate in sequence. The lever system engaging the wheel. Camera watches the first dry-run of the chronograph: the start lever trips, the column wheel rotates one position, the chronograph seconds hand sweeps its first second at 18,000 vph. The mechanism works. The test is passed.
00:02–00:04.5 · THE CASE AND CRYSTAL
Speed 50%. The 42mm stainless case machines itself — the asymmetric case with its crown and pushers on the left. Each pusher threads into its tube. The hesalite acrylic crystal — chosen over sapphire for its behavior in vacuum — descends and pressure-fits into the bezel. Camera underneath looking up through the crystal as it seats — the watch dial now visible through it, slightly warm in color.
00:04.5–00:07 · MISSION CONTROL
Speed 15%. The Speedmaster — fully built — sits on a metal desk under fluorescent lights. A hand in a NASA flight suit sleeve reaches in and straps it to a wrist over a pressure suit. Camera macro on the deployment clasp latching. Then: the wrist lifts and the camera follows — we're in Mission Control, Houston, 1969. Banks of screens, engineers in white shirts and ties. The wrist checks the Speedmaster against the mission clock on the wall. 3… 2… 1…
00:07–00:10 · LAUNCH
Speed 8%. The chronograph pushers — both of them — pressed simultaneously by a gloved thumb. The seconds hand begins sweeping. The camera pulls back as the room shakes — Saturn V launch vibration. The Speedmaster on the wrist, perfectly steady, counting. The only thing not shaking is the watch and the hand wearing it.
00:10–00:13 · THE MOON
Speed 3% — absolute slow motion. A boot descends onto lunar regolith — the dust pluming upward in lunar gravity's lazy arc. The Speedmaster on the wrist above, visible in frame. Camera rotates slowly to face the watch: the tachymeter bezel reading mission elapsed time. The lunar surface in background, Earth hanging above the horizon. No sound. Absolute silence. The seconds hand sweeps.
00:13–00:14.5 · THE REVEAL
Speed 2%. The Speedmaster floating in the void of space — Earth below, stars above. Every surface element reads: the black dial, the three sub-registers, the hesalite crystal's warm distortion at the edges, the steel case catching starlight. The chronograph still running. Mission: ongoing.
00:14.5–00:15 · END CARD
Hard cut to black. Omega Seahorse. "The first watch worn on the moon." No grain — this moment is crystal clear. Then: one frame of grain. Cut.
